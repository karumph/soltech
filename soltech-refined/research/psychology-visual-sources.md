# Soltech visual and interaction psychology: source notes

Research date: 2026-09-28. Research only; no interface code or browser interactions changed.

Scope supplied for this review: mobile-first Soltech; white and silver glass surfaces; restrained blue, lilac, and teal; Coin Checker uses real data; scanners currently support setup and are not live. These are project premises, not independently audited here.

## What the evidence supports

Soltech can have a recognizable visual identity while preserving recognizable controls. Separate visual distinctiveness from navigation novelty, and measure both brand recognition and task success. Favorable appearance, credible appearance, actual accuracy, and continued use are different outcomes. The eight primary sources below do not establish a recipe that guarantees all four.

## Eight primary sources

### V1. First impressions: complexity and recognizable structure

**Tuch, A. N., Presslaber, E. E., Stöcklin, M., Opwis, K., & Bargas-Avila, J. A. (2012).** “The role of visual complexity and prototypicality regarding first impression of websites: Working towards understanding aesthetic judgments.” *International Journal of Human-Computer Studies*, 70(11), 794–811. DOI: 10.1016/j.ijhcs.2012.06.003.

- [Author institution publication page](https://research.google/pubs/the-role-of-visual-complexity-and-prototypicality-regarding-first-impression-of-websites-working-towards-understanding-aesthetic-judgments/); [original preprint, mirrored PDF](https://uxgu.ru/ext/static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en/us/pubs/archive/38315.pdf).
- **Method/sample:** Two screenshot-rating experiments, recruiting 59 and 82 participants. Study 1 used 119 analyzed website screenshots with 50/500/1,000 ms exposure; Study 2 used 80 with 17/33/50 ms exposure. Main outcome: perceived beauty. Separate samples calibrated screenshot properties.
- **Finding:** Lower visual complexity and greater resemblance to the expected website category favored first-impression appeal. Complexity influenced very brief judgments; category typicality became more influential with longer exposure.
- **Limits:** Static screenshots, predominantly university-associated participants, historical websites. This was not a test of completion, bounce rate, retention, or brand memorability. Exposure duration is not a deadline by which every visitor decides to leave.
- **Soltech inference:** Keep task hierarchy immediately readable; preserve familiar search, navigation, and disclosure patterns while making the surrounding visual language distinctive.

### V2. Aesthetic polish does not substitute for experienced usability

**Tuch, A. N., Roth, S. P., Hornbæk, K., Opwis, K., & Bargas-Avila, J. A. (2012).** “Is beautiful really usable? Toward understanding the relation between usability, aesthetics, and affect in HCI.” *Computers in Human Behavior*, 28(5), 1596–1607. DOI: 10.1016/j.chb.2012.03.024.

- [Publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563212000908); [original paper, university-hosted copy](https://cpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/wp.wwu.edu/dist/8/2868/files/2018/04/Tuch-et-al-2012-Is-Beautiful-Usable-2don1em.pdf).
- **Method/sample:** 80 participants used one of four online-shop versions in a 2×2 manipulation of aesthetics and usability, found specified products, and supplied pre/post-use ratings with validated instruments. Information architecture manipulated usability without changing the page layout.
- **Finding:** In this experiment, aesthetics did not improve perceived usability. Poor usability reduced subsequent aesthetic ratings; affective experience was implicated in this relationship.
- **Limits:** One task/domain and a strong usability manipulation. This does not establish that aesthetics never affects perceived or actual usability; the paper explicitly discusses mixed prior findings. No longitudinal return behavior.
- **Soltech inference:** Evaluate finding a token, understanding results, and saving a scanner separately from “looks premium.” Frustrating labels and misleading states can undermine a polished first impression.

### V3. Novelty and familiarity can coexist

**Hekkert, P., Snelders, D., & van Wieringen, P. C. W. (2003).** “‘Most advanced, yet acceptable’: Typicality and novelty as joint predictors of aesthetic preference in industrial design.” *British Journal of Psychology*, 94(1), 111–124. DOI: 10.1348/000712603762842147.

- [Publisher abstract](https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1348/000712603762842147).
- **Method/sample:** Three empirical studies relating consumer-product typicality, novelty, and aesthetic preference; one examined expertise, and another measured typicality through average similarity to category members. Participant counts were not verified from accessible primary full text; only the publisher abstract was relied on.
- **Finding:** Novelty and typicality jointly predicted preference while statistically suppressing each other. A preference for recognizable category membership is compatible with a preference for novelty.
- **Limits:** Physical-product judgments, not mobile navigation, and statistical associations do not prescribe a universal optimal mixture. No Soltech brand-recognition, usability, or retention outcome.
- **Soltech inference:** Use original surface composition, signature geometry, and a coherent voice around ordinary task mechanics. “Familiar” need not mean visually interchangeable with competitors.

### V4. Recognition and liking are distinct brand-design objectives

**Henderson, P. W., & Cote, J. A. (1998).** “Guidelines for Selecting or Modifying Logos.” *Journal of Marketing*, 62(2), 14–30. DOI: 10.1177/002224299806200202.

- [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/002224299806200202); [original article PDF, university-hosted copy](https://newion.uwinnipeg.ca/~ssingh5/Lm/logo-guide.pdf); [accessible original-article transcription used for methods](https://paperzz.com/doc/8003756/guidelines-for-selecting-or-modifying-logos).
- **Method/sample:** 195 unfamiliar real logos, assessed on 13 design characteristics by separate student/design-expert samples. Student rating variables averaged 56 respondents each (47–70). Recognition involved two-second exposures, testing after about ten minutes and again one week later; recognition-group total N was not verified.
- **Finding:** Design attributes related differently to recognition, affect, and familiar meaning. The authors’ high-recognition profile combined naturalness, harmony, and moderate elaboration.
- **Limits:** Exploratory ratings/regressions of logos, not randomized whole-interface identity systems. Correctly recognizing a seen logo differs from identifying its company and from returning to a product. Repetition *inside* a logo is not a test of repeated branding across screens.
- **Soltech inference:** Test identifying Soltech among matched alternatives and recalling it later; preference ratings alone cannot show ownership of a distinctive look.

### V5. Legibility: direct performance evidence for light reading surfaces

**Piepenbrock, C., Mayr, S., Mund, I., & Buchner, A. (2013).** “Positive display polarity is advantageous for both younger and older adults.” *Ergonomics*, 56(7), 1116–1124. DOI: 10.1080/00140139.2013.790485.

- [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23654206/); [author institution full paper](https://www.psychologie.hhu.de/fileadmin/redaktion/Oeffentliche_Medien/Fakultaeten/Mathematisch-Naturwissenschaftliche_Fakultaet/Psychologie/AAP/Publikationen/2013/Piepenbrock-2013-Positive_display_polarity_is_.pdf).
- **Method/sample:** 84 younger adults (18–33) and 85 older adults (60–85), with condition-specific exclusions. Between-subject light/dark polarity comparison using visual acuity and proofreading tasks on an LCD.
- **Finding:** Dark characters on a light background improved acuity and proofreading performance in both age groups. Reading rate did not differ significantly.
- **Limits:** Controlled lighting, high-contrast black/white stimuli, desktop screen, and exclusions for relevant eye diseases. It does not establish a universally best theme, a font-size rule, or benefits from low-contrast gray text on translucent glass.
- **Soltech inference:** White/light data surfaces are defensible; use solid, readable text and measured contrast. Decorative silver treatments should not reduce number, label, or status legibility.

### V6. Motion can help explain changes, with task-specific limits

**Heer, J., & Robertson, G. G. (2007).** “Animated Transitions in Statistical Data Graphics.” *IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics*, 13(6), 1240–1247. DOI: 10.1109/TVCG.2007.70539.

- [Author lab publication page](https://idl.uw.edu/papers/animated-transitions); [author lab full paper](https://idl.cs.washington.edu/files/2007-AnimatedTransitions-InfoVis.pdf).
- **Method/sample:** 24 participants completed two controlled experiments comparing static, animated, and staged transitions: tracking objects and estimating changing values. Animated transitions lasted 1.25 or 2 seconds depending on task.
- **Finding:** Animation improved tracking and some value-change judgments. Benefits varied by chart/transition; stacked-bar estimates showed no significant advantage, and aggressive staging could perform worse than simpler animation.
- **Limits:** Constrained chart-reading tasks, not perpetual background motion or general mobile microinteractions. The tested durations are not recommended timings for Soltech buttons. Stated preference does not prove repeat use.
- **Soltech inference:** Reserve motion for understandable state transitions and spatial continuity, then test whether it helps. Avoid gratuitous moving backgrounds, recurring entrance delays, or animation that falsely implies a scanner is running.

### V7. Actual speed has behavioral evidence

**Brutlag, J. (2009).** “Speed Matters for Google Web Search.” Google, June 22, 2009.

- [Original technical report](https://services.google.com/fh/files/blogs/google_delayexp.pdf); [author report on Google Research](https://research.google/blog/speed-matters/).
- **Method/sample:** Live experiments injected 50–400 ms server-side delays at different stages of result delivery for four or six weeks, compared with controls. Sample size is not disclosed in this short report.
- **Finding:** Added delays of 100–400 ms corresponded to approximately 0.2–0.6% fewer daily searches per user over the experiment. Usage effects could deepen over time and persist after the delay ended.
- **Limits:** Industry report, not a fully specified peer-reviewed trial; historical Google search behavior. Searches per user is a usage measure, not necessarily satisfaction or returning-user retention. Magnitudes should not be transferred to Soltech.
- **Soltech inference:** Keep interactions responsive and data delivery efficient. Do not hold completed results to finish a brand animation. Immediate press acknowledgement and honest loading/error states are sensible design proposals, but this experiment specifically tested actual delay, not their independent effects.

### V8. Polish can increase perceived credibility without validating content

**Robins, D., & Holmes, J. (2008; online 2007).** “Aesthetics and credibility in web site design.” *Information Processing & Management*, 44(1), 386–399. DOI: 10.1016/j.ipm.2007.02.003.

- [Publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0306457307000568); [original article PDF](https://makinggood.ac.nz/media/1276/robinsholmes_2008_aestheticsandcredibilityinwebdesign.pdf).
- **Method/sample:** Convenience sample of 20 students, 14 women and 6 men. Participants judged 42 screenshots: high/low aesthetic versions of 21 sites with the same content, presented in counterbalanced sequence. Average credibility response took about 3.2 seconds.
- **Finding:** Higher aesthetic treatment generally increased perceived content credibility.
- **Limits:** Small convenience sample; credibility ratings from screenshots, not checking factual correctness, accomplishing tasks, purchasing, or returning. A design bundle changed, not a single color or isolated styling detail.
- **Soltech inference:** Polish increases the responsibility to make evidence visible. Real Coin Checker results need source/freshness/context; scanner configuration must be clearly distinguishable from live monitoring. Attractive presentation cannot validate unavailable functionality.

## Proposed Soltech signature system — design hypotheses, not research findings

These choices respond to the requested identity and should be evaluated with Soltech users. None of the studies above proves that a particular hue, shape, texture, font, or motif will work for this product.

1. **One recognizable motif:** Explore a restrained “signal aperture” or cut-ring geometry derived from the Soltech mark. Use a consistent distinctive cut angle, contour, and proportion in the mark, selected control, and occasional section anchor. Avoid inventing unfamiliar navigation icons to carry the brand. Check actual differentiation against adjacent products before choosing it.
2. **A consistent material treatment:** White reading planes, shallow silver edging, and a small number of glass overlays. The signature should survive in screenshots and reduced-motion mode. Keep factual data on optically stable surfaces; reserve translucency for chrome where it remains readable.
3. **A disciplined color allocation:** One recognizable blue action accent; lilac and teal used for defined secondary roles. Use labels/icons alongside status color. This is an identity and information-mapping proposal, not “blue causes trust” or “teal means safety.” Test actual contrast and interpretation.
4. **A typography and number system:** A distinctive heading/wordmark treatment paired with highly readable operational text; consistent tabular numerals, decimal precision, units, timestamps, and hierarchy. Recognition should come from repeated proportions and details, not decorative type inside addresses or dense figures.
5. **A small movement vocabulary:** A brief change in the signature edge or selection indicator can acknowledge a real state change. Data reveal should occur when ready. Any pulse suggesting live monitoring must correspond to an actual live condition; scanner setup should communicate “configured” or “saved,” as appropriate.
6. **A consistent voice:** Short, specific language tied to the action and evidence. Personality can come from precision, occasional restrained warmth, and a recurring visual detail. Empty states should explain what happens next. Avoid celebrating hypothetical profit or presenting unsupported certainty.

“Childish,” “generic AI,” and “premium” are audience judgments, not universally measurable style categories. Excessive novelty, decorative clutter, conflicting motifs, and irrelevant motion are plausible risks; declaring all gradients, round corners, mascots, or bright accents unprofessional would exceed this evidence. Compare candidate designs with the intended audience and ask for reasons.

## Measurement plan: preserve the distinctions

| Question | Appropriate observation | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does this look appealing and relevant? | Brief exposure followed by appeal, category, and expected-purpose judgments | Successful task completion or retention |
| Is the look recognizably Soltech? | After controlled exposure, identify Soltech among visually comparable distractors; repeat after a delay | Ability to use the product or desire to return |
| Does it look credible? | Credibility rating before use, with reasons | Accuracy, safe investments, or operational readiness |
| Can users complete the task? | First-attempt success, errors, time, unnecessary backtracking, and correct explanation of the result | Long-term usage |
| Is the system state understood? | Ask whether a configured scanner is currently monitoring, and what data freshness means | Backend correctness unless independently verified |
| Does the experience encourage useful return? | Actual later usage/cohort return after the needed functionality is available | Causality from a simple before/after visual redesign |

Test at realistic mobile widths and on slower connections. Hold copy, data, functionality, and exposure reasonably constant when comparing visual alternatives. Include a recognition question with the logo hidden to assess whether the broader identity system carries the brand; treat this as a proposed test, not a standard validated by V4. Gather spontaneous descriptions before offering words such as “premium” or “childish.”

For Soltech, useful task examples are: check a token, locate an evidence/source detail, explain a missing or stale value, configure and save a scanner, and correctly identify that scanner's current operational status. Optimize the signature system only while those tasks remain clear. A follow-up retention test becomes meaningful when there is a real recurring job the product can perform.

## Evidence boundaries

- This is a focused primary-source review, not a systematic review or proof that the cited effects generalize to contemporary mobile crypto tools.
- Processing fluency is a useful interpretation of simple, recognizable presentation, but V1 does not independently measure fluency as the causal mechanism. Do not turn it into a claim that all effort or all novelty is harmful.
- None of these studies supplies universal emotional meanings for blue, lilac, teal, white, or silver, or proves glass styling is more trustworthy.
- Consistent use of signature assets is an implementation hypothesis grounded in distinguishable identity cues; the logo study does not directly test a whole-app consistency system.
- No first-impression or aesthetic-preference result here proves completed work or retention. The strongest observed ongoing-use evidence in this set concerns actual latency (V7), with the stated domain and reporting limits.
