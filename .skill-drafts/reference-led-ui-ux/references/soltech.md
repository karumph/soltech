# SolTech visual brief

Use only for SolTech work. These notes capture the user's supplied brand direction and their preference for small previews guided by real examples. They do not authorize site edits, new screens, backend work, external publishing, or replacing an accepted design.

## Identity

Preserve the supplied logo. Its connected, outward-facing dual profiles, faceted geometry, symmetry, and black-and-white contrast are sources of subtle visual cues. Inspect the actual asset in the current project before using it. An existing asset was found at `dist/assets/soltech-logo.png` within the SolTech UI project; do not assume every future checkout uses that path.

Use the clarity, hierarchy, and care associated with Apple-inspired simplicity while making a distinct SolTech identity. Avoid an Apple copy, a generic dashboard, or a recolored template. Do not repeat faces, fill backgrounds with polygons, or force every component into angular or mirrored forms.

## Palette and components

Start from black, white, charcoal, silver, and cool grays. Explore one signature accent, potentially a refined blue; its exact hue is a design choice, not a fixed requirement. Keep brand accents distinct from warning, error, and success colors. Aim for crisp, inviting contrast without washed-out surfaces or neon effects.

Use readable type, deliberate spacing, clear hierarchy, consistent icons, and restrained comfortable rounding. Content should determine composition. Scanner cards, result lists, and detail views may have different structures while sharing typography, color roles, and component details. Do not place everything in identical oversized cards.

Use clear information surfaces. Shadows, layering, occasional glass, and short purposeful transitions may support hierarchy. Logo-inspired paired details are optional experiments, not a quota. Avoid decorative animation, excessive blur, glow, random gradients, and generic futuristic styling. Respect reduced-motion preferences.

## Working style

The user reports that broad requests such as making the interface better have missed their intent. Find relevant visual examples proactively, explain a few concrete choices, and present a small preview they can react to. Preserve the parts they like. Reference research should reduce guessing without turning every minor adjustment into an approval process.

The latest request and actual project state determine functional scope. Do not infer mock-only or live-data behavior from old prototypes. Do not invent prices, subscription limits, trading functions, risk guarantees, or scanner capabilities to fill the design.

The original brief explicitly allows judgment: usability and cohesion take precedence over incorporating every stylistic suggestion. Judge recognizability through a few consistent choices rather than placing the logo everywhere.
